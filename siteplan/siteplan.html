1. Site Name

Accra Street Eats

This name was chosen because it tells visitors exactly what the site is about (street food) and where it is focused (Accra, Ghana). It is short, easy to remember and easy to read on a phone. The tagline is: Your guide to the popular street food of Ghana and the people who make them.

Optional domain idea: accrastreeteats.com (availability not checked).

2. Site Purpose

Accra Street Eats is a three-page informational website that helps locals, tourists and members of the Ghanaian diaspora discover and choose popular Ghanaian street foods such as waakye, kelewele, bofrot, kenkey and chichinga (khebab).

  • Home page: a short introduction to the street food scene, featured dishes and clear navigation to the other pages.
  • Food Guide page: a list of dishes, loaded from a JSON file and filtered by type (breakfast, snack, etc.), with a description, ingredients, typical cost and a photo for each dish.
  • Where to Eat page: popular places in Accra to find each dish, hygiene tips, what to order, and a simple form to recommend vendors.

The site will use valid and accessible HTML and CSS, a responsive layout, optimized images, JSON data and browser APIs such as local storage.

3. Scenarios

These questions come from the target audience and drive the content of the site.

  1. I am a tourist visiting Accra for the first time. Which street foods should I try first, and how much do they usually cost?
  2. Where in Accra can I find the best waakye or kelewele, and which places are clean and safe to eat from?
  3. What is in kenkey or bofrot, and is it a breakfast food or a snack?
  4. I am part of the diaspora and miss home. Which dishes can I look up to remember what I grew up eating?
  5. I know a great food vendor. How can I recommend them to other visitors?

5. Typography

Two fonts from Google Fonts are used, each with a fallback of Arial and then a generic sans-serif.

  • Poppins (600 weight): headings (h1, h2, h3), site name, navigation and table headers. It is bold and friendly, which suits a food brand.
  • Open Sans (400 and 600 weight): body text, lists, captions and forms. It is very readable on small screens.

Poppins: Waakye, Kelewele, Bofrot

Open Sans: Ghanaian street food is affordable, delicious and part of everyday life in Accra.
